Sagar Accounts Software Integration with JAI Agency Operational POS & Inventory

1. Executive Summary
During system integration testing, the Sagar Accounts Software was connected directly to JAI Agency (the primary operational POS and Inventory system).

The Core Architectural Principle
JAI Agency remains the sole operational source of truth for day-to-day business operations: counter sales, product cataloging, inventory batches, storage procurement, retail customer payments, counter expenses, and sales returns. Accounts does not create a duplicate operational database or competing inventory system.
Accounts Software functions as the unified financial and double-entry general ledger engine. It automatically ingests operational business events, applies accounting rules, enforces double-entry invariants ($\sum \text{Debits} = \sum \text{Credits}$), maintains financial period controls, and dynamically generates statutory reports (Trial Balance, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, GST, and Tax Ledgers).
Manual Accounting Entries in Accounts handle organizational accounting events that JAI Agency POS has no awareness of: fixed asset capitalization, depreciation, bank commercial loans, owner capital injections, owner drawings, and administrative overheads (rent, electricity, salaries, bank charges).

2. What the Complete System Does
The integrated architecture provides complete separation of responsibilities between operations and financial governance:

Transaction Ingestion: Ingests operational events from JAI Agency SQLite database (JAI_AGENCY.db) into the Accounts double-entry database (accounts.db).
Idempotency & Deduplication: Employs cryptographic SHA-256 payload hashing within accounting_sync_registry to ensure that running synchronization once, twice, or a hundred times never generates duplicate financial entries.
Double-Entry Enforcement: Every transaction generates a balanced journal voucher where Total Debits equal Total Credits. Unbalanced entries are rejected before touching the ledger.
Subledger & Control Account Synchronization: Real-time alignment between individual customer/supplier subledgers and their respective General Ledger control accounts (1040 Accounts Receivable and 2010 Accounts Payable).
Periodic Financial Reporting: Generates standard dynamic accounting statements (Trial Balance, P&L, Balance Sheet, GST Tax Return summary, and Bank Reconciliation worksheets) computed directly from the immutable journal ledger lines.

36. Duplicate Protection
To guarantee that sync operations never create duplicate journals, the system implements an idempotent sync pipeline:

Fingerprint Computation: For every incoming record (e.g., Sale Bill #3), the sync engine extracts business fields (id, date, total, items, customer) and computes a SHA-256 hash.
Registry Lookup: The engine checks accounting_sync_registry for a matching (source_module, source_entity, source_id).
Behavior:
First Sync: Record not found $\rightarrow$ journal created, posted to GL, hash stored in registry with status = 'POSTED'.
Second Sync (Identical Data): Hash matches registry $\rightarrow$ operation skipped, logged as synced = 0, skipped = 1.
Third Sync: Skipped again. Zero duplicate journals created.

39. Manager-Friendly Explanation
Answers to common questions from management:

What is JAI Agency responsible for?
JAI Agency is the operational front-end for counter billing, POS checkouts, inventory batch arrival, and walk-in customer interactions.
What is Accounts responsible for?
Accounts is the financial accounting engine that maintains double-entry books, handles balance sheets, tracks assets, loans, equity, and generates statutory reports.
Which data comes automatically from JAI Agency?
Sales bills, sales returns, customer credit settlements, storage purchase invoices, supplier payments, and daily counter expenses.
Which data must be entered manually in Accounts?
Fixed asset capitalization, depreciation entries, bank loans, loan EMI payments, founder capital contributions, personal drawings, and central administrative overheads (rent, staff salaries, bank fees).
What happens when a sale is created?
Accounts debits Cash/Bank/UPI (or Accounts Receivable for credit sales), credits Sales Revenue, and credits Output GST Payable.
What happens when inventory is purchased?
Accounts debits Raw Material Consumption (COGS), debits Input GST Credit, and credits Bank (for cash purchases) or Accounts Payable (for credit purchases).
What happens when inventory is sold?
JAI Agency deducts stock. In Accounts, revenue is recognized against the cost base established by inward purchases, ensuring matching on the P&L.
What happens when a customer pays?
Cash, Bank, or UPI increases, and Accounts Receivable decreases. No revenue is created again.
What happens when a supplier is paid?
Accounts Payable liability decreases, and Bank Account balance decreases. No expense is created again.
What happens when an expense is entered?
The designated operating expense account debits (increasing expenses on P&L), and Cash on Hand or Bank Account credits.
How are GST and TDS handled?
Every transaction automatically splits tax into Output GST (collected) or Input GST (paid). The tax engine calculates net liability without manual math.
How are assets and depreciation handled?
Assets are capitalized on the Balance Sheet. Periodic depreciation moves a portion of the asset value into P&L expenses over time.
How are loans handled?
Disbursal increases bank balance and loan liability. Repayments split into principal reduction (reducing liability) and interest expense (P&L overhead).
How are capital and drawings handled?
Capital increases equity; drawings reduce equity directly on the Balance Sheet. Personal withdrawals do not inflate business expenses.
How are AR and AP calculated?
AR is the sum of unpaid customer invoices. AP is the sum of unpaid supplier invoices. Both reconcile to their respective GL control accounts.
How are final balances generated?
Every account balance is computed from the sum of debits and credits across all posted journal lines in the general ledger.
How do P&L, Trial Balance, and Balance Sheet get their values?
Statements are generated dynamically from general ledger lines: $$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{COGS}$$ $$\text{Net Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses}$$ $$\text{Assets} \equiv \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$$
How do we prevent duplicate transactions?
A sync registry tracks every record by a SHA-256 fingerprint. Repeating a sync simply skips already-processed transactions.
How do we know the accounting is balanced?
The double-entry engine enforces balance on every entry. The Trial Balance confirms: Total Debits (₹588,590.00) = Total Credits (₹588,590.00), with ₹0.00 discrepancy.
